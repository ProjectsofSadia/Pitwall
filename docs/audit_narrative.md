# Audit narrative

Hand-written interpretation of the Phase 0 measurements. Kept separate from the
generated `DATA_AUDIT.md` so that rerunning the audit refreshes the tables
without destroying the analysis. Section headings here must match section
headings in the generated document; `scripts/run_data_audit.py` substitutes each
block for the corresponding placeholder.

Written against the run recorded in `data/audit/run_manifest.json`
(FastF1 3.8.1, config SHA-256 `acbb6f2a...`).

## Lap Timing Data

All 31 columns declared in the design are present, with no expected column
missing. 1135 lap rows across 19 drivers.

Three coverage facts matter downstream. Driver 23 (ALB) has no lap data at all,
which is why the laps table carries 19 drivers against 20 in the results.
Driver 18 (STR) has a single row, generated by FastF1 rather than observed
(`FastF1Generated` = 1.0), with no lap time and no position, so the effective
racing field is 18 cars from lap 2. Attrition then removes COL and HUL around
lap 30 and SAI around lap 39, leaving 15 cars for the final third.

Compound labelling is clean: `frac_compound_missing` is 0.0 for every driver and
only INTERMEDIATE and WET appear. The pre-declared failure criterion for
unreliable compound labels did not trigger, and compound remains OBSERVED rather
than requiring reconstruction from stint boundaries.

`frac_is_accurate` sits at 0.81 to 0.84 for every driver who completed the race,
which is high for a wet race with four neutralization periods. The exception is
STR at 0.0, already flagged in the manifest.

The per-lap `TrackStatus` distribution confirms that the field is a
concatenation, not a code: 807 laps (71.1%) are pure green, but 154 are `12`,
26 are `21`, 15 are `671` and one lap is `1267`, meaning that lap contained
green, yellow, VSC deployed and VSC ending segments. Any eligibility filter that
compares this field for equality against a single code is wrong, and the
consequences are quantified under Wetness Index Feasibility.

## Track Status

31 transitions, all with populated session-time stamps, decoded without any
unknown code appearing. The log is time-resolved to the millisecond and is
therefore sufficient for the regime-dependent pit-loss model the design
requires.

The neutralization sequence, read directly from the table:

- 6578.99 s, yellow (approx lap 27)
- 6618.85 s, VSC deployed (approx lap 28)
- 6707.99 s, VSC ending
- 6721.71 s, all clear (approx lap 29)
- 6890.20 s, safety car (approx lap 30)
- 7159.33 s, red flag (approx lap 32)
- 8592.33 s, all clear, after a 1433 s suspension
- 9312.05 s, safety car (approx lap 39)
- 9686.30 s, all clear (approx lap 42)

Session status corroborates the suspension independently: Aborted at 7159.01 s,
Started at 8637.65 s, a 1478.6 s gap. The two sources agree to within the
expected offset between the red flag being shown and the session being formally
aborted.

The interval between VSC ending at 6707.99 s and green at 6721.71 s is 13.7
seconds. Two pit entries fall inside it. That interval is the entire reason this
project models pit loss on session time rather than lap number.

## Race Control

107 messages, all placed on the session time axis after the timestamp fix. The
category breakdown is 58 `Other`, 16 sector-scoped CLEAR, 14 sector-scoped
DOUBLE YELLOW, 5 SafetyCar, 5 sector-scoped YELLOW, 3 track-scoped CLEAR, 2
track-scoped GREEN, and one each of RED, BLUE and DRS.

Note that VSC deployment and withdrawal do not appear as a distinct category or
flag; they are inside the 58 free-text `Other` messages. A parser will therefore
be required, and it must be tested rather than written inline. Race control is
usable as corroboration for neutralization timing, but `track_status` remains
the primary source because it is structured and already time-resolved.

## Weather

201 samples, zero nulls in every channel, median sampling interval 60.002 s
(range 59.77 to 60.22 s) across a 12000.7 s span. Ranges: air temperature 20.4
to 23.4 C, track temperature 23.2 to 29.5 C, humidity 75 to 92%, wind speed 0.0
to 1.8 m/s.

**Rainfall intensity is not available from FastF1 weather data.** The `Rainfall`
channel has dtype `bool` with exactly two unique values and is true in 58.2% of
samples. This was measured, not assumed, and it is the empirical basis for the
project's decision to treat weather state as a derived field wetness index
rather than as a rainfall variable.

Two further limits on this data. It comes from a single trackside sensor, so it
carries no spatial information about a circuit where rain routinely falls on one
sector and not another. And at one sample per minute against lap times of 82 to
102 s, the effective resolution is roughly one observation per lap, which is
adequate as a covariate and inadequate as a state.

## Telemetry

Car data is available for all 20 entries with the documented channels present.
Measured sample intervals are consistent with the documented ~240 ms. The
`Date` column is used for all duration measurement, since FastF1 documents
`Time` as inaccurate with duplicate values; `Time` is used only to window
against session-relative event timestamps, and that mismatch is recorded in the
`window_source` column of the stationary probe output.

## Position Data

Position data is available for all 20 entries. FastF1 emitted incomplete-data
warnings for driver numbers 241, 242 and 243, which correspond to no entry in
the laps table and are artifacts of the position stream rather than cars. They
are recorded in the manifest and have no effect on any Phase 0 measurement.

The only real driver number present in `pos_data` but absent from laps is 23
(ALB), consistent with that car having no lap data.

## Pit Stop Observability

36 pit events detected. After classification, 17 are red-flag suspensions, 2 are
retirements with no out-lap (COL and HUL, both on lap 30), and **18 are usable
stops**.

The exclusion is not cosmetic. Suspension rows carry `pit_lane_duration_s`
between 1283 and 1426 s. Fitted over the unfiltered column, a duration
distribution has a mean of 680 s and a 75th percentile of 1420 s. Over the
usable 18, the median is 25.81 s with an interquartile range of 25.32 to 27.97 s.

**Pit lane duration is close to invariant across neutralization regimes.**
Medians by regime at pit entry: green 26.41 s (n=8), VSC deployed 25.98 s (n=7),
VSC ending 25.19 s (n=2), safety car 28.73 s (n=1). This confirms that the
neutralization discount does not live in the stop. It lives in the time the car
would otherwise have spent on track, which is inflated when the field is slowed.
Net pit loss must therefore be computed against a contemporaneous field
reference and can never be read off the stop duration.

**19 of 36 stops straddle a track status change.** The decisive cases are on
lap 28:

| driver | pit entry (s) | status at entry | seconds to next change | status at exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RUS | 6717.87 | VSC ending | 3.84 | all clear |
| NOR | 6719.16 | VSC ending | 2.55 | all clear |
| TSU | 6734.55 | all clear | 155.65 | all clear |
| LAW | 6753.27 | all clear | 136.93 | all clear |

Four stops, one lap number, three regimes. Russell and Norris entered the pit
lane with under four seconds of VSC remaining and exited under green. Tsunoda
and Lawson entered fully under green, 15 and 34 seconds later. A lap-resolution
model assigns all four the same pit-loss regime and is wrong about at least two
of them, at the pivotal moment of the race.

**Stationary time is separable from pit lane transit.** All 8 probed stops show
a clear halt reaching 0.0 km/h: HAM 2.60 s, LEC 2.60, ALO 2.72, PIA 2.76, HUL
2.96, SAI 3.04, COL 3.16, BEA 13.44.

The BEA outlier provides an internal consistency check. His pit lane duration was
35.91 s against a roughly 25.5 s norm, an excess of 10.4 s. His stationary time
was 13.44 s against a roughly 2.9 s norm, an excess of 10.5 s. Two independent
measurements of the same anomaly agree to within 0.1 s, which is evidence that
the decomposition is real rather than an artifact of the windowing.

## Gap Reconstruction

Gaps are reconstructed from start/finish line crossing session times within a
lap group, using only information available at the moment of crossing.

The reconstruction is sound for this race. `frac_order_matches_position` is 1.0
on 67 of 69 laps, and `cars_not_on_leader_lap` is 0 on every lap: no car was
lapped at any point, so the same-lap restriction never binds. The two exceptions
are lap 1, where classified position reflects the start rather than a completed
lap, and lap 39, where the safety car was deployed mid-lap and crossing order
legitimately diverges from classification.

Median gap ahead grows from about 1.0 s in the opening stint to 4.6 s by the
finish, with minimum gaps frequently below 0.5 s. Clean-air thresholds therefore
matter a great deal: at 1.5 s the eligible sample is 555 car-laps, and the
count falls sharply at 3.0 s.

One limitation that the numbers cannot show. A fixed time gap is a poor proxy
for clean air in the wet, because spray affects a following car much further
back than it would in the dry. The threshold is exposed in configuration and
carried into the sensitivity analysis rather than being treated as settled.

## Wetness Index Feasibility

This section records the finding that changed the methodology.

**Outside neutralization the index is well supported.** Under the relaxed
eligibility chain, W(t) is observable on 58 of 69 laps against a declared floor
of 4 clean-air cars. The signal is large and coherent: field median clean-air
lap time runs 84.2 to 85.4 s through laps 17 to 26, reaches 102.26 s on lap 29,
and recovers through 97.76, 91.52, 88.82, 87.01 and 86.08 on laps 34 to 38
before settling near 84.5 s from lap 45 and drifting to 82.0 s by lap 65 as the
track dries and fuel burns off.

**Inside neutralization it is not merely difficult, it is undefined.** Eleven
laps yield no observation: 1, 27, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33, 39, 40, 41, 42. These form
two contiguous blocks and both are caused by neutralization, not by rain.
FastF1's `IsAccurate` already excludes safety car, VSC and red-flag laps, and
under VSC and safety car drivers run to a delta time, so their lap times carry
no information about grip even if they were retained. Green-flag field pace
cannot be measured while there is no green flag.

The first blackout block, laps 27 to 33, contains the decision this project
exists to study.

**Lap 29 is an island that arrives too late.** Between the VSC clearing at
6721.71 s and the safety car at 6890.20 s there are 168 seconds of green. That
single lap yields 11 eligible cars, 9 in clean air, and a median of 102.26 s
against 85.45 s on lap 26: a deterioration of 16.8 s per lap. It is the first
measurement of how bad conditions had become, and it is timestamped after
Russell and Norris had already committed to their stops.

**A leading indicator does exist before the blackout.** Cross-driver dispersion
rises before the median does. Interquartile range of clean-air lap times runs
0.32 to 0.76 s through laps 17 to 24, then 0.76 on lap 25 and 1.79 on lap 26,
while the median moves only from 84.62 to 85.45 s. Conditions were deteriorating
measurably two laps before the VSC, and dispersion showed it earlier than
central tendency. This is available at the decision point and is not hindsight.

**The strict filter was discarding real data.** Requiring an exact per-lap
`TrackStatus` of `1` excluded whole laps over a three-second sector yellow.
Relaxing to exclude only neutralization codes, matching FastF1's own
`IsAccurate` criterion, recovers 143 wet-compound car-laps (800 to 943) and 81
clean-air car-laps at the 1.5 s threshold (474 to 555). Individual laps recover
substantially: lap 4 from 0 to 7 eligible, lap 11 from 1 to 8, lap 36 from 0 to
13, lap 38 from 2 to 13, lap 43 from 0 to 4, lap 44 from 1 to 7, lap 51 from 1
to 10. Crucially, the relaxed chain rescues none of the eleven neutralized laps,
confirming that the two effects are distinct and that the blackout is structural
rather than an artifact of an over-strict filter.

Both chains are retained in the output so the comparison is an artifact rather
than an assertion. Proper yellow handling, using `SectorNSessionTime` to
establish whether a car was actually in the affected sector during the yellow
window, is deferred to Phase 3.

## Temporal Resolution

The project operates on session-relative seconds throughout, referenced to
`t0_date` = 2024-11-03 14:38:02.746.

Resolution by source: track status and session status are event-driven and
millisecond-stamped; race control messages are absolute UTC timestamps converted
through `t0_date`; lap and sector crossings are per-lap and per-sector session
times; weather is one sample per 60.00 s; car telemetry is roughly 240 ms and
position data roughly 220 ms.

FastF1 does not use one timestamp convention across its session tables.
`weather_data`, `track_status` and `session_status` parse `Time` with
`to_timedelta` and are session-relative. `race_control_messages` parses the
message `Utc` field with `to_datetime` and is absolute. An initial
implementation assumed uniformity and failed on the real session. The converter
now dispatches on dtype and refuses to convert an absolute timestamp without a
reference, because epoch seconds would sort, difference and merge without
complaint while being wrong by decades.

The binding constraint on the decision engine is not resolution but observation
frequency. Track status resolves to milliseconds, which is finer than any
decision needs; the wetness observation resolves to roughly one usable
measurement per lap, and to none at all during neutralization.

## Missingness and Reliability

No expected column is absent. Missingness in the laps table is low and
structurally explicable: `Position` 0.18%, `LapTime` 3.35%, sector times 3.26%,
sector session times 3.44%, compound and tyre life 0.0%. `PitInTime` and
`PitOutTime` are 96.8% and 96.9% null, which is correct rather than a defect:
they are per-stop events recorded on a per-lap table. Weather has no nulls in
any channel.

Two reliability findings are worth recording.

**Reproducibility, verified.** The audit was executed three times against the
same session under FastF1 3.8.1, each from an empty cache with a fresh download.
Two of those runs were compared output by output: the strict-chain wetness
table, pit events, gap diagnostics, driver lap counts and track status are
byte-identical between them. The third was compared on its manifest, which
agrees on every row count, on both configuration hashes, and on all derived
summary fields.

**Version sensitivity, observed.** An earlier execution under FastF1 3.8.3
produced 1134 lap rows and 18 drivers, with both driver 23 and driver 18 flagged
all-laps-inaccurate. FastF1 3.8.1 produces 1135 lap rows and 19 drivers, with
only driver 23 flagged. The same session read by two patch releases of the same
library therefore differs in row count and in data-quality flags. No claim is
made here about which is more correct. The project pins 3.8.1, the version under
which every result in this document was produced, and the loader now warns into
the manifest whenever the installed version differs from the pin.

FastF1 degrades on partial data failures by logging rather than raising, so its
warnings are captured into the provenance record. This run captured five: no lap
data for driver 23, the consequent accuracy-check failure, and incomplete
position data for the non-existent driver numbers 241, 242 and 243.

## Data Classification

47 variables resolved against the loaded session with **zero mismatches**. Every
variable declared OBSERVED is present (27 of them), every variable declared
NOT_AVAILABLE is confirmed absent from all loaded tables (7), five DERIVED
quantities were produced by this audit, and eight are deferred to later phases.

The seven confirmed-absent variables are rainfall intensity, rain radar, team
weather forecast, fuel load, tyre temperature, tyre pressure and brake
temperature. None is approximated anywhere in this project. Fuel effect appears
separately as ASSUMED rather than as a measurement, and is carried into the
sensitivity analysis rather than fixed.

Leakage risk is derived from classification by a single rule rather than written
per variable, so the rule is auditable in one place. Every OBSERVED session
table is high risk if read directly and must be accessed through the temporal
gate; every DERIVED quantity additionally requires an expanding estimator
window; every corpus-ESTIMATED quantity requires São Paulo 2024 to be held out.

## Known Limitations

Stated plainly, in rough order of how much they constrain the conclusions.

1. **The weather state is unobservable at the decision points.** The eleven
   neutralized laps include the entire first decision window. W(t) at lap 28 is
   a forecast from lap 26, not a measurement, and its uncertainty is
   correspondingly wide.
2. **Rain intensity does not exist in the public data.** The rainfall channel is
   boolean, from one sensor, once a minute, with no spatial resolution on a
   circuit where rain is routinely local.
3. **Teams had information this project cannot have.** Radar, proprietary
   forecasts, driver radio and tyre telemetry were all available on the pit wall
   and are all absent here. The system is not reconstructing what a team knew;
   it is asking what was inferable from public observation.
4. **Eighteen usable pit stops is not a pit-loss dataset.** Regime-conditional
   estimates require the multi-race corpus. Estimates from this race alone would
   be uncertainty theatre.
5. **The red flag is a rule, not a distribution.** A 1433 s suspension with a
   free tyre change and a restart cannot be represented as a long pit stop, and
   a hazard estimated from one occurrence is not a probability.
6. **Tyre state is not observable.** Compound and age are known; temperature,
   pressure and actual degradation are not. Degradation will be inferred from
   lap time, which conflates it with fuel burn, track evolution and driver
   effects.
7. **Fuel load is assumed, not measured.** A constant burn rate and a constant
   time-per-kilogram sensitivity are both approximations.
8. **Clean air is proxied by a time gap.** In the wet, spray extends the
   interference distance well beyond any threshold calibrated on dry running.
9. **One car contributes nothing and another is absent entirely.** STR has a
   single generated lap, ALB has none, so the field is 18 from lap 2 and 15 from
   lap 40.
10. **Counterfactual outcomes are unverifiable in principle.** No amount of data
    quality changes this. It is why the validation strategy targets component
    calibration, forced-scenario replay fidelity, robustness of the action
    ranking and comparison against naive baselines, rather than claiming the
    counterfactual distributions are ground truth.

## Implications for Methodology

Four consequences follow from the measurements, and all four are binding.

**W(t) becomes a filtered state, not a per-lap measurement.** This is a change
from the Phase 0 design and is recorded as such. The observation model is field
median clean-air fuel-corrected pace, available only on green laps meeting the
declared floor of 4 eligible cars, which holds on 58 of 69 laps. The state model
is a local linear trend whose innovation variance is estimated from the corpus.
Across a neutralization the state is propagated with no observation and its
variance grows with the gap. At lap 28 the belief is a forecast from lap 26,
roughly 100 seconds of race time stale, with honestly wide uncertainty.

Three auxiliary observations survive neutralization and can be folded in without
leakage: the rainfall boolean, race control wet-track messages, and the compound
choices other cars are making. The last is genuinely informative here. PER
fitted wets on lap 27, TSU and LAW on lap 28. That is contemporaneous public
evidence of the field's revealed belief about conditions, and using it is
observation of the present, not knowledge of the future.

The consequence is that the model's uncertainty at the pivotal decision will be
large. That is the correct result, not a deficiency, and it makes "the actions
cannot be confidently distinguished under the available uncertainty" a live
conclusion.

**Pit loss must be modelled on session time, and net loss needs a field
reference.** The lap 28 stop sequence settles the first point empirically. The
regime invariance of pit lane duration settles the second: the discount is in
the field, so net loss is the pit lane duration minus what the car would have
taken to cover the same ground on track, evaluated under the regime in force at
that instant.

**The multi-race corpus is now a prerequisite, not an enhancement.** Eighteen
usable stops, one red flag, two safety cars and one VSC cannot support
regime-conditional pit loss, neutralization hazards, overtaking calibration or
the wetness innovation variance. Phase 0.5 must complete before Phase 4.

**The eligibility filter is relaxed and both chains are retained.** Neutralization
codes exclude a lap; yellow does not. Sector-aware yellow handling is deferred to
Phase 3, where sector session times can establish whether a car was actually in
the affected sector.
